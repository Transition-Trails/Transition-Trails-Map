Four certifications, ninety applications, two first-round interviews

Marcus finished a two-year business degree at community college with no idea what came next. Entry-level postings wanted three years of experience or had quietly been automated away. So he did what everyone told him to do: he collected certifications. Each one felt like progress. None of them changed the outcome.

The turn

What actually changed, and it was not knowledge

Marcus arrived knowing more about the platform than some working admins. Twelve weeks later he was hired. Nothing was added to what he knew — four things were added to what he could show.

Someone else's mess
Every certification exam gives him a clean scenario. Guided Trail gave him an org nobody documented, with overlapping rules and permissions granted in the wrong place. Reading a system you did not build is most of the actual job and no exam tests it.
A reason for every choice
Twelve Decision Log entries: what he chose, what he rejected, and the business reason. An interviewer can pick any one of them and probe it. That is the difference between claiming judgment and evidencing it.
Ninety seconds, twelve times
His first recorded answer was thirty seconds of nothing. By week twelve he could explain a technical decision to a non-technical person on demand. The improvement curve is itself the artifact — and it is the skill that gets him past a screening call.
People who vouch for him
A coach who watched him work for twelve weeks, three squad members who tested his builds, and a nonprofit that uses what he delivered. He applied for job ninety-one with references, which he had never had before.
Marcus did not find a program on LinkedIn — he found a monthly Trail Talk, sat in the back of it for free, and heard people describe the exact wall he was hitting. That is the honest entry point for someone who has already been sold three things that did not work. Trail Talk costs him nothing and asks nothing, which is precisely why he showed up. Then Explorer's Trail gave him somewhere to be while he got ready: daily quests, a monthly mission, group coaching, and prior capstones to build against — practice with company, instead of another exam alone at a kitchen table.
