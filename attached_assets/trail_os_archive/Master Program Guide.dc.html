01 · The pathway

Four learner programs, one client program, and the gate between each one

The gates are what make the pathway mean anything. A learner does not move because they finished — they move because something external says they are ready. Explorer's Trail sits underneath all of it rather than in sequence: it is where someone waits, where they stay once placed, and where they come back between engagements. Digital Compass sits on the other side entirely — its members are nonprofit organizations, and it is where the real work the last two programs need actually comes from.

What opens when
1 October, or as soon as twenty people are on the waitlist. Explorer's Trail and Guided Trail open together — the first official Guided Trail cohort starts at whichever comes later. Twenty is the number that makes a cohort of three to five viable with room for the people who are not ready yet.
Foundations Trail — no published date. It is in Cohort 0 testing and the site says so plainly, with a waitlist and nothing promised. An already-certified applicant enters at Guided Trail anyway, so Foundations is not on the critical path to a first placement — which means there is no cost to deciding late and a real cost to announcing a date we then move.
Trail of Mastery — waitlist only, Cohort 0 in 2027. Interested people are being collected, not enrolled.
Explorer's Trail opening alongside Guided Trail matters more than it looks: it is where waitlisted people wait. Without it, twenty interested people have nowhere to be while the cohort fills — and the ones who came in through a Trail Talk are exactly the ones who drift.
Certification gates entry, evidence gates progress. You need the Platform Foundations exam to start and the Platform Administrator exam to reach Guided Trail — but nothing inside a program is passed by examination. It is judged against published criteria. Nobody starts over. Vocabulary, Decision Log numbering, coverage records and the portfolio all continue across programs. If a boundary makes a learner relearn something, that is a defect in our design.

05 · Evidence

Judged, not graded

Three verdicts, the same three in every program. No letter grades, no curve, no points. A coach names the criterion that was unmet, and the learner resubmits. The skill assessment carries the question of whether they improved.

Pass
Every criterion met. Not excellent — met. A coach who wants to say more says it in the coaching, not in the verdict.
Needs rework
The work exists and a named criterion is unmet. The expected state for a first attempt at judgment work — and the falling rate across sprints is the evidence of learning.
Not attempted
Nothing was submitted. Recorded plainly, without commentary, and reported rather than suppressed.
Where it all lives
Existing objects only: LMS for enrollment and modules, Program Management for engagements and sessions, Case and its related records for client work. A verdict is a child record on the learner's LMS enrollment — one row per criterion, carrying artifact, verdict, coach level, date, and rework sequence — sitting alongside Program Engagement. That is what makes a rework curve queryable rather than reconstructed.
Twelve artifact types cover all twelve weeks of Guided Trail, each with its own pass criteria and named failure modes. A coach learns twelve rubrics once rather than a new one each week — see Guided Trail — Coach Rubric.
06 · Measures

Six figures, defined once, computed the same way everywhere

This is the specification the dashboard should be built against. Identical definitions across programs are what let a report say "across our programs" instead of reporting three times and hoping nobody asks why the numbers differ.

{{ m.name }} {{ m.def }} Source{{ m.source }}
Foundations is measured toward an exam and an apprenticeship toward client delivery, so their pass rates are not comparable and must never be summed. Digital Compass is further out still — it measures organizations rather than people, on its own nine figures, and belongs in a separate panel again. The dashboard should make all of that structural rather than editorial, because a reviewer who finds an unstated apples-to-oranges comparison stops trusting the whole document, and one who sees you flag it yourself stops looking for others. The single figure that spans both halves is Learner Pipeline — the share of Digital Compass project slots filled by learners rather than staff — and it fails if either side is not working.
