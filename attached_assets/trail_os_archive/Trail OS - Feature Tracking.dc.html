FOUND 5 AUGUST

The trail field on Contact holds two different formats

Five learners carry a trail value, and it is recorded three ways: one as a hyphenated identifier, two as display labels. The trail configurations are keyed on hyphenated identifiers.

explorer-journey — 1 learner, matches a configuration
Guided Trail — 3 learners, no match
Trail of Mastery — 1 learner, no match

The consequence is that Penny's trail context layer silently fails for four of five learners. A learner whose trail reads as a label finds no configuration, so Penny answers them without knowing their program, their phase or her role for that trail — and nothing reports a problem, because an absent layer is a normal condition.

Two fixes, and both are worth doing. Normalize the existing values so every learner resolves. Then constrain the field, because a free-text or loosely-valued trail will drift again — and this is the third time a value mismatch has produced a silent failure rather than an error.

Resolved — per-learner progress lives on two objects the workbook never listed

Settled 4 August. The missing parent object turned out not to matter: production already carries a course enrollment object and an activity completion object, and the completion object is where a learner's own progress lives. The assignments query has been rebuilt onto it.

One structural detail worth keeping: the link runs enrollment to completion, not contact to completion. There is also a denormalized contact field on the completion object, which is what makes a per-learner query possible without a join.

Neither object carries a due date. The endpoint now reports that plainly so the interface can suppress the column rather than render blanks. If a due date is added later, the wiring point is marked in the code.

Resolved — learner identity comes from a Google group

Settled 4 August. A dedicated learner group will grant access to the learner surface, the same mechanism already driving staff access. Contact is still where a learner's record lives, but group membership is what makes someone a learner. This unblocks the live learner read model.

Two identities per learner, deliberately — and they need joining

Clarified 6 August, and it is a design decision rather than an accident. Contact holds how they entered the program — admissions, trail, cohort, coach. User holds what they did in it — cases assigned, work performed, activity in the org. Two different questions, two different records, which is a cleaner split than forcing both onto Contact.

Case assignment uses an existing custom field pointing at User, so the case list resolves through the User side while the learner profile resolves through Contact.

Decided 6 August: a User lookup on Contact. Contact stays the record a person starts from, and the lookup reaches their activity — so a single query can return entry and activity together, which is what both the homebase and any coach view of a learner need.

Populated during a staff-facilitated onboarding step, agreed 6 August — a person sets it when the license is provisioned, alongside the Google group that grants access. Grouping the two is worth doing deliberately: they are the same moment, and separating them is how a learner ends up able to sign in but unable to see their own work.

Two things still to settle. An unset lookup should render as "not yet linked" rather than "no cases" — different truths, only one honest, and the first tells whoever ran onboarding that a step was missed. And whether anything needs to traverse the other way, from a User back to their Contact, which the lookup alone does not give you.

Learner onboarding — the staff-facilitated setup

Agreed 6 August, and it does not need inventing — an Action Plan in Salesforce already lists the onboarding steps. Use it as the process rather than writing a second checklist that will drift from it. Three things must be on it: the Salesforce license issued, the User record linked to their Contact, and the Google group added.

Agentforce runs it with the employee — reading the plan, checking what is actually done rather than what is ticked, and flagging the gap. That is the same pattern as everywhere else: Agentforce retrieves and verifies, the person decides.

The verification half is the valuable part. A tick means someone said they did it; a check means the license exists, the lookup is populated and the group contains them. Any one missed leaves a learner partly set up — signed in but with no cases, or licensed but locked out — and each failure looks different enough that nobody would connect them to the same session.

Penny hands record lookups to Agentforce

Confirmed 6 August as the general rule, not a one-off. Anything a learner asks for that resolves to records — cases, Knowledge articles, verdicts, Decision Log entries, logged time, curriculum — Penny routes to Agentforce, which returns the records. Generation is reserved for coaching.

Three consequences worth building to: no feature ever needs its own search field; a new record type is a new Agentforce action rather than a new screen; and cost tracks coaching rather than usage.

Two constraints to hold. A retrieval must be visibly a retrieval, so nobody mistakes a returned record for something Penny composed. And Agentforce runs as the learner, so results are bounded by their permissions rather than by prompt instructions.

Absence handling — Penny prepares, the coach approves

Agreed 6 August, out of the learner homebase work. When a learner has been away, the deadlines they return to should already have been moved — the learner should never come back to a page full of things that went red while they were ill.

Penny watches for the absence and prepares the adjustment; the coach approves it. Same pattern as the rest of the coach view.

Three things it needs before it can be built: how many missed days counts as an absence, which dates Penny may propose moving, and who handles the client-facing half — telling a nonprofit their deliverable has moved is a person's judgment, not an automation.

Penny suggests skills from the unmatched queue

Added 6 August to the volunteer and staff pages. Penny offers short things to learn that unlock work — drafting marketing copy, editing a Build with Me video, Flow basics.

The suggestions come from the queue, not from a catalog. That is what makes this different from a training menu nobody opens. If four cases asked for marketing copy and none found a taker, that is the skill worth offering — and the work is already waiting when someone finishes. The same shape as the Knowledge-gap flag: the system tells you what it is short of rather than guessing what you might enjoy.

Each suggestion pairs a short format with a real first job, and the staff version deliberately favors removing bottlenecks over adding capacity — a second person who can write a Penny prompt is worth more than a third who can do what two already do.

Learning time counts toward the monthly commitment (agreed 6 August). A volunteer who spends an hour learning video editing has contributed that hour, because next month they can take work nobody could take this month. Staff log the same as development time. This is the detail that makes the feature real rather than aspirational — asking someone giving eight hours a month to spend one of them unpaid on training would simply mean nobody does it.

Points come from closed cases only, not from learning. Two currencies again, for the same reason as the Cairn: hours measure contribution, points measure delivered work. Learning earns the first and not the second, so nobody can build a merch balance without finishing anything.

Concurrent case limits — three for a volunteer, five for staff as drawn, both configurable. The reason stated in the interface is the honest one: a limit exists so a case does not sit with someone who ran out of time for it. Worth deciding whether the cap sits on the person record beside the hours agreement, since both come from the same conversation at onboarding.

Two things still to resolve. Where the content comes from — Trail Kits already exist and are the obvious source, which would mean volunteers and staff using the same kits sold externally. And whether completing one updates their specialty, because if it does the queue starts matching them automatically, and that loop is the whole point.

Step recorder — one capture, four outputs

The skeleton already exists in Trail OS: record screen, upload image or add a blank step, then title, describe and annotate each one, reorder by drag, export. It replaces Tango in the stack. Entry points are now on all three homebases.

The same mechanism, four different artifacts. Staff record a process and it becomes an SOP article. A coach records a walkthrough for someone stuck, and it becomes a teaching article the whole squad can find rather than a conversation only one learner heard. A learner records a demo of what they built — that one is different in kind, because it is evidence a coach judges and a portfolio piece the learner keeps. And a nonprofit-facing recording becomes client documentation.

The pipeline it completes. Recorded steps feed the Video Production flow already built — script, voice, produce — which is exactly the Build with Me format Trail Kits are sold on. So a Trail Kit stops being authored from a blank page and starts being produced from a recording of someone doing the thing. That is the strongest argument for finishing this before anything else in content.

Three things to resolve. Article type by audience — the record type should follow from who recorded and why, not be picked from a list. Who reviews — a staff SOP and a learner demo need different approval, and a learner demo probably needs none at all before their coach sees it. And where recordings live, since a screen recording of a dev org is not the same sensitivity as one of a client org.

Advocacy — building the audience through the people already here

Raised 6 August, open. Learners, coaches and volunteers earn points for engaging with Transition Trails content on LinkedIn and for posting about their own work, redeemable against merch made only for participants. For volunteers this is likely the main quest type; for learners it sits alongside the Cairn.

Why this is defensible, and worth saying out loud in the copy. A learner writing about the process map they built for a food bank is doing two things at once — building the organization's audience, and building the public evidence an employer will look for. That alignment is what separates it from asking unemployed people to do free marketing, and the framing should lead with the learner's benefit rather than ours.

Keep it a separate currency from the Cairn. Stones measure judgment practiced; advocacy points measure reach contributed. Merging them would let someone build a strong-looking trail by posting rather than thinking, which quietly corrupts the one signal the whole program rests on. Two currencies, both visible, only one feeding a verdict.

Three things to resolve. Verification — LinkedIn's API will not tell us who engaged, so this is self-reported with a link, and it needs a light check rather than an honour system that rewards the loudest. Quality over volume — points for a real post about real work, not for a daily "great insight" comment, or the feed fills with noise under our name. And what merch actually is, since exclusivity is the whole appeal and a generic branded item earns nothing.

Four views of the same page, not two

Confirmed 6 August: learner, coach, internal employee, and volunteer. The homebase shell is shared — Workspace drawer, Penny as the single input, a collapsible band at the top, work in the middle, people on the right — and what fills it differs by audience.

Two things sit high on every one of the four: the log-time row, directly under Penny's band, and the recorder. Time is the most frequent action any audience takes and was buried on the first pass; the recorder differs only in what it produces. Both belong in the shared shell rather than being placed per page.

The volunteer view is a variant of internal staff (decided 6 August), so Penny needs no new identity — volunteers use the internal one, which already exists and is the only identity currently built. That is the cheapest of the four to ship.

What differs is the page rather than the voice: a volunteer is not on a trail, has no verdicts, but does log hours and may hold cases. The Cairn band is replaced by a monthly contribution band, and their equivalent of a coach is the Volunteer Coordinator, assigned on their record the same way a learner gets a coach and a coach gets a Lead.

The monthly commitment is per person, not a constant. Eight hours was never universal — it is whatever was agreed at onboarding, so it belongs on the volunteer record and set in the same staff-facilitated session as the licence, the User link and the Google group. Hard-coding eight would misreport everyone who agreed to something else, and the page would quietly tell them they were behind when they were not.

Learner view

Trail OS with Slack alongside as the communication layer, likely in a side panel. Trail OS is where the work and coaching happen; Slack carries conversation, nudges and quest delivery.
