The pivot itself

Why analyst, then owner, is the right order

A career pivoter's instinct is to aim straight at the senior title. For someone with a decade of frontline domain knowledge, going Business Analyst first is faster — not slower — because it converts what she already knows into the artifacts a team runs on. Product ownership comes after, when she has earned the right to decide rather than only to describe.

Analyst first, because she already knows the truth
A BA finds out what is actually happening in a process. Denise has been inside one for ten years. The skill she is missing is not curiosity — it is writing what she knows as a testable requirement someone else can build from.
Owner second, because deciding is different
A BA finds out what is true; a Product Owner decides what happens because of it, and says no to the rest. Sequencing under uncertainty and refusing a stakeholder are not research skills, and they only develop where there are consequences.
Her old job is the interview answer
"I managed a support team for four years, and here is the case-routing decision I made on a real nonprofit engagement — and what I said no to." A pivoter with both halves is more credible than a graduate with one, and the portfolio is what makes the second half checkable.
A BA who is fluent in a process can start answering questions from memory instead of asking them — the trap of the expert analyst. Her Guided Trail findings register and her Product Owner Decision Log both exist partly to catch it: every claim about what the client does has to point at something she verified in their org, not at what was true at her old employer.
What carries across

One continuous record, four years long

Nothing restarts at a program boundary. A learner is not enrolling over and over — they are walking one trail, and the evidence accumulates the entire way.

The Decision Log
Entry #1 in Week 1 of Foundations, numbered continuously and never restarted. By the Product Owner track it is where she records what she refused and why — the artifact a hiring manager reads first.
The public portfolio
One shareable page she owns for as long as she stays involved — hosted here, not trapped in a sandbox that expires. For a pivoter it does the heaviest lifting: it is the proof that the second half of her résumé is real.
Explorer's Trail
Not a stage between programs — the connective tissue underneath all of them. Earned at Guided Trail completion and never given up, it is where she waits for a role, brings real questions once she has one, and stays between engagements.
Expedition Weekend
Twice a year, every program, one sealed brief opened together on a clock — one virtual, one in person in Dallas. A learner in month two and a coach in year three build side by side, and recruiters watch the work happen.
RESOLVE
Recognize · Explore · Select · Outline · Launch · Verify · Evolve. A module in Guided Trail, and the method both apprenticeships run on end to end — so her BA work and her PO work share one spine.
Penny
Primary coach in Foundations, the interviewer in Guided Trail, the goal-setter in Explorer's Trail, and the stakeholder who changes her mind mid-sprint in the Product Owner track. One coach who has read everything Denise has ever submitted.
