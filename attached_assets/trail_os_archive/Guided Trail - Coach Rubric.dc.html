Guided Trail · coach rubric

Pass, rework, or not attempted

No grades. Every judgment names the criterion it was made against and the artifact it was made from, so the record holds up to a funder reading it cold twelve months later.

01 · The three states

What each one means, and what a coach does next

✓Pass
Every criterion for the artifact is met. Not excellent — met. A pass says a competent practitioner would accept this from a colleague.
Coach action: record it and move on. Praise belongs in the campfire, not the rubric.
⟳Needs rework
The work exists and one or more criteria are unmet. The coach names which, in one sentence, and the learner resubmits. Rework is the expected state for a first attempt at judgment work — it is not a soft failure.
Coach action: name the unmet criterion, set a resubmit date inside the sprint. No unlimited loops — two rework cycles, then it goes to the sprint gate as-is.
—Not attempted
Nothing was submitted. Recorded plainly and without commentary. This is the state that matters most to a funder, because a program that never records it is not measuring anything.
Coach action: a conversation about capacity, not curriculum. Two in a sprint triggers the completion review.
This is criterion-referenced assessment — a learner is measured against a fixed standard, not against the rest of the cohort. That is the form funders and accreditors trust, because it cannot be inflated by an easy cohort or deflated by a strong one. A B-minus tells a funder nothing. "Met nine of eleven criteria, reworked two, both passed on resubmission" tells them exactly what happened.
02 · What makes it defensible

Four rules that hold the rigour up

Every criterion is observable
A criterion must be something two people can look at the same artifact and agree on. "Names the option rejected and why" is observable. "Shows good judgment" is not.
Test: could a second coach, with no knowledge of the learner, reach the same verdict from the artifact alone? If not, rewrite the criterion.
Every verdict cites its evidence
The record stores the artifact, the criterion, the verdict, the coach, and the date. A verdict with no artifact behind it is an opinion.
In Salesforce: a record, not a spreadsheet cell — so it is reportable, and Penny can read it when drafting a session agenda. See section 04b for where each piece lives.
Rework is counted, not hidden
A program where nothing is ever reworked is not assessing. Report the rework rate as a feature: it is the visible evidence that the standard is real.
Expect: a healthy first cohort reworks somewhere between a quarter and a half of judgment artifacts. Near zero means the criteria are too loose.
The coach judges the artifact, Penny scores the knowledge
Domain scores come from the skill assessment. Pass and rework come from a human reading work product. Neither substitutes for the other, and the separation is what makes the improvement claim credible.
Consistent with: the Absolute Rule in your assessment framework — Penny surfaces data, the coach makes the call.

How weekly verdicts roll up

When a squad buddy finds a defect, the finding goes on the record and no verdict is attached to the builder for it. The builder fixes and resubmits, and the resubmission carries the Pass or Needs rework. This is deliberate: if a finding counted against the builder, learners would learn to test their partners gently — and the honest test is the entire reason for pairing them. The tester’s own Pass comes from the quality of their peer test report, not from how much they found.
Learners do not hold the client relationship — the coach assigns the work and reviews before anything reaches a production org. So judge what the learner controlled: did they refine the requirement, name the risk, write a testable script, respond to the buddy’s findings, and request release cleanly. A deferred release is not a rework trigger unless the reason was something the learner should have caught.
04b · Where the record lives

Salesforce holds it, which is why the claim survives an audit

All of it is tracked on existing objects — LMS, Program Management, and Case with its related records. That matters for rigour: a funder asking how a figure was produced can be shown the record, not a spreadsheet someone maintained by hand.

What is recordedWhere it livesWhat it has to carry
Attendance and participation Program Engagement, per session attended Live or replay, so the two-replay limit is queryable rather than remembered

Peer test findings and release requests Related to the Case the work belongs to Tester, date, what was observed, and the coach release decision with its reason
The rubric verdict itself A child record on the learner’s LMS enrollment, alongside Program Engagement Artifact, criterion, verdict, coach, date, and rework sequence number — one row per criterion
This is the detail the whole funder claim rests on. If a verdict stores only "Needs rework" against an artifact, the rework rate is reportable but nothing else is. Storing a verdict per criterion means you can say which criterion learners miss most across a cohort — and that is a curriculum finding, not just a grade. It is also what lets the rework curve be honest: the same criterion, measured in Sprint 1 and Sprint 3.
05 · Proving improvement

Three measures, three different claims

Knowledge — the skill assessment
Domain scores at intake and at Week 12, same instrument, same exam-domain weighting. Movement per domain, reported by domain rather than as a single number.
Claim it supports: "Learners improved in the domains the certification measures."
Judgment — the rework curve
Rework rate on judgment artifacts, sprint over sprint. A cohort that reworks 40% of Sprint 1 recommendations and 10% of Sprint 3 recommendations has demonstrably learned something the exam does not test.
Claim it supports: "Learners reached professional standard on work product, and we can show the trajectory."
Articulation — first and twelfth answer
The same 90-second format, twelve times. Two coaches score the first and last against the same three criteria without knowing which is which.
Claim it supports: "Learners can explain their work to an employer" — the outcome the program actually sells.
what you can defend
Of five cohort members, four completed all twelve weeks and one withdrew in Week 6 after being hired. Across 61 assessed artifacts, 70% passed first submission, 25% were reworked once and then passed, 3% were reworked twice, and 2% were not attempted. Rework on stakeholder recommendations fell from 47% in Sprint 1 to 13% in Sprint 3. Mean domain score rose from 68% to 84%, with every learner improving in at least six of nine domains.
Illustrative numbers, real structure. Every figure in that paragraph comes from a record with an artifact behind it — which is the difference between reporting outcomes and asserting them.
