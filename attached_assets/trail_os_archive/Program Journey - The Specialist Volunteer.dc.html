She trades what she knows for what she needs

Simone is a marketing manager — eight years of campaign strategy, segmentation, and demand generation. The promotion she wants requires something her day job will not give her: building the campaigns herself in Salesforce, and pulling numbers out of Salesforce, Google Analytics, and three other systems without asking anyone. We need marketing help. She needs hands on the platform. So we trade.

The exchange

What each side actually gets

Written plainly, because a vague volunteer arrangement is the kind that quietly ends. Both columns have to be worth it after month three, not just at signup.

She gives
·Eight hours a month — about two a week, with room to miss one
·Marketing automation capability we have no budget to buy
·Delivery on real nonprofit and internal work, coach-reviewed like anyone else's
·Eventually, the design of a track only a practitioner could write
She gets
·Explorer's Trail membership for as long as the commitment holds
·Self-guided learning, daily quests, group coaching, prior capstones
·Nonprofit work on her public portfolio — the part her day job cannot give her
·A route past her ceiling: coaching, then an apprenticeship in a new domain
The number is eight hours a month. Roughly two a week, expressed monthly on purpose so a bad week or a holiday does not put her in breach of an agreement she is honouring in spirit. It is small enough to survive a busy quarter and large enough to be real. Skill is not coaching. Eight years of campaign work does not make her calibrated to your rubric — she goes up the same ladder as everyone else: Coach's Assistant first, verdicts confirmed, then Associate Coach. Still open: what happens when the hours lapse. Decide whether membership pauses, converts to paid, or continues on goodwill, and say it at signup rather than improvising in month five.
