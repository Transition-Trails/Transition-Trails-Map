1. Program Overview

1.1 What Is the Digital Compass?

The Digital Compass is Transition Trails' nonprofit client technology program — the program arm that serves nonprofit organizations directly rather than individual learners. It provides structured Salesforce guidance, technology training, and consulting project support for nonprofit professionals who need to use their tools more effectively but don't have a dedicated technology staff member to rely on.

The Digital Compass is not a course. It is a membership — a recurring relationship between Transition Trails and the nonprofit organizations we serve. Members get weekly access to live expert support, a growing library of self-serve resources, and the ability to submit real Salesforce and technology projects to be completed by coached learners from the Guided Trail and Trail of Mastery programs.

The program is already running. Digital Compass Office Hours have been active since early 2026, serving Hartford Promise, Global One Urban Farming, and other nonprofit clients. This blueprint formalizes what is working and defines the structure for scale.

The Digital Compass Difference: Real Help for the Person Who Didn't Ask for This Job

The Accidental Admin didn't apply to be a technology lead. They applied to run programs, manage donors, or support their community. Then Salesforce got installed — and suddenly it was their job.

The Digital Compass exists for that person. Not a certification program. Not a bootcamp. A standing door that is always open, with real people on the other side who have been where you are and know how to help.

Weekly Office Hours. TrailKits built from real client work. Projects completed by coached learners. And a capstone experience that lets you practice in a safe environment before you make changes in your live org.

You are not behind. You are exactly where we expected you to be. Let's go.
 |

1.4 Trail OS — The Technology Foundation

The Digital Compass requires the installation of Trail OS — Transition Trails' proprietary app — in the member organization's Salesforce org. Trail OS is what makes the Digital Compass relationship work technically. It provides Transition Trails with the access, visibility, and tooling needed to support, assess, and complete project work inside a member's org safely and effectively.

Trail OS installation is completed by the Transition Trails technology team as part of member onboarding. Members do not need to configure or manage Trail OS themselves. The installation process is documented, supported, and completed before any project work begins.

Why Trail OS Matters

Without Trail OS, every project starts from scratch. With Trail OS, Transition Trails has the foundation needed to assess your org, assign the right learner to your project, and deliver consistent, documented work.

Think of it as the connection that makes the partnership real. You open the door. We make sure we can actually help once we're inside.

2.3 Weekly Office Hours

All Digital Compass members — at every tier — have access to Weekly Office Hours. This is the heartbeat of the program. It is a live, open session where nonprofit professionals can bring real questions, walk through real problems in their real org, and get real answers from the Transition Trails team.

Office Hours are not a class. They are not a webinar. They are what the name says — an open door. Show up with a question. Leave with an answer.

What Gets Covered in Office Hours

From active Digital Compass sessions, here is what members have brought:
  •  Salesforce basics for non-technical staff — reports, views, tasks, opportunities
  •  Tech stack guidance: which tools to use together (Salesforce + QuickBooks + Google Workspace + Slack)
  •  HR system scoping: ATS selection, payroll integration, recruiting workflows
  •  Training on specific modules: Volunteer Management, Event Management, NPSP
  •  Grant deadline tracking in Salesforce
  •  Roadmap planning for nonprofit tech expansion
  •  Any question you've been embarrassed to ask anyone else

No question is too basic. That is not a platitude. It is the policy.

3.1 What Is the HCH Capstone?

The Horizon Community Hub (HCH) Capstone is a safe-practice Salesforce environment that Digital Compass members can use to build confidence, develop skills, and try things without fear of breaking their live org.

HCH is the same fictional nonprofit used across Transition Trails' learner programs — a Dallas-Fort Worth organization providing utility assistance, housing stabilization, and veteran services to families in crisis. In the learner programs, participants play the role of an incoming Salesforce admin. In the Digital Compass capstone, the perspective shifts. You are not the admin building the system. You are the nonprofit professional who has to use it, manage it, and make decisions about it.

The Capstone Perspective Shift

In the Guided Trail: You are the Salesforce Administrator building HCH's system from scratch or inheriting it as an admin.

In the Digital Compass Capstone: You are the Program Director, Development Officer, or Executive Director who has to use that system — understand what it's doing, ask the right questions, and make decisions about what needs to change.

Same org. Different chair. Different skills. Both matter.
 |

4. TrailKits — Self-Serve Learning Resources

4.1 What Are TrailKits?

TrailKits are Transition Trails' self-serve digital resources — built from real client work with Hartford Promise, Global One Urban Farming, CLYLP, and other nonprofit partners. Every TrailKit is a complete package: a guide or template, a set of AI prompt packs tuned to the specific use case (Claude, Gemini, and GPT versions), and curated nonprofit discounts for the tools the kit requires.

TrailKits are not courses. They are not tutorials. They are what you hand to someone who needs to get something done today — built by practitioners who did the same work themselves in real orgs.

All Digital Compass members have access to the TrailKit library as part of their membership. New kits are added as they are completed.

4.2 Fix It With Me & Build With Me Videos

In addition to written TrailKits, Digital Compass members have access to two types of video resources, both narrated by Penny, Transition Trails' AI coaching persona:

Video Type
 | What It Is
 |

 | Screen-recorded walkthroughs of common Salesforce problems — the ones nonprofit admins encounter most often and don't know how to diagnose. Hugh records the screen. Penny narrates. You watch, follow along, and fix the same issue in your own org.
 | 
Build With Me
 | Step-by-step configuration walkthroughs for setting up specific Salesforce features, modules, or workflows from scratch. Designed for the nonprofit staff member who has been asked to set something up and needs to see exactly how it's done before touching their live org.

The Penny Layer

All video content in TrailKits is narrated by Penny — Transition Trails' AI coaching persona. Hugh records the screen. Penny narrates. Angela reviews and approves.

This is intentional. When Salesforce updates its UI, only the screen recording needs to change — the audio swaps in minutes. Penny on video, Penny in Slack, and Penny as an AI prompt persona creates a coherent, multi-modal coaching experience across every Digital Compass touchpoint.

Mid and Full tier Digital Compass members can submit project requests to be completed by Transition Trails learners from the Guided Trail and Trail of Mastery programs. This is the core value exchange of the Digital Compass ecosystem — real nonprofit technology problems, solved by coached and supervised learners, at a fraction of the market cost.

Projects are scoped, assigned, and delivered through a structured process managed by the Transition Trails operations team. Every project has defined deliverables, a timeframe, and a project plan. Members know what they will receive and when.

It is not a training program. It is a membership community that delivers real technology support through a model that benefits everyone involved.

Traditional Salesforce consulting firms deliver project work but at costs that exclude the majority of small to mid-sized nonprofits the Digital Compass is designed to serve. 7. Completion & What Comes Next

7.1 What Membership Delivers Over Time

The Digital Compass does not have a graduation date. It is a membership — a sustained relationship between Transition Trails and the nonprofit organizations we serve. What members gain compounds over time: each Office Hours session builds on the last, each project completed leaves the org in a better state than it was, and each TrailKit applied reduces the time it takes to solve the next problem.

The measure of success is not a credential. It is a nonprofit organization that uses its technology more confidently, serves its mission more effectively, and spends less time fighting its tools.

7.2 The Member Technology Roadmap

Mid and Full tier members receive a technology roadmap as part of their annual Architecture Review. This roadmap is a living document — updated annually, informed by Trail OS data, and prioritized by impact and capacity. It is the primary tool members use to decide which projects to submit, what to tackle in Office Hours, and where to invest their technology budget.

The roadmap is produced by Hugh and Kim following the Architecture Review and delivered in a format the member can share with their board, their funders, and any Salesforce partner they work with.

7.3 Connecting the Dots — From Digital Compass to the Full Ecosystem

Digital Compass membership is sometimes the beginning of a deeper Transition Trails relationship. Nonprofit staff who begin attending Office Hours sometimes discover they want to go further — not just use Salesforce, but build a career in it. When that happens, the pathway is clear.

A Final Note on Why the Digital Compass Exists

Nonprofit organizations are doing the most important work in our communities. And too many of them are doing it with technology that doesn't work the way it should — because they can't afford the consultant, can't find the training, and can't carve out the time to figure it out alone.

The Digital Compass exists to close that gap.

Not with a course. Not with a certification. With a standing door that is always open — weekly office hours, real project support, resources built from actual client work, and a team that has been where you are.

You don't have to know what you're doing yet. You just have to show up.

— Kim Barnes, COO, Transition Trails
