Purpose of This Document
This framework defines how Penny and Trail OS assess learner Salesforce skills across all program tracks. Every assessment is grounded in Salesforce's published certification exam domains. Every gap surfaces to the right person at the right time. No coach has to guess who is struggling — the system tells them. This document is the specification for that system.
 | 

SECTION 1 — THE CORE PRINCIPLE
 | 

Every Track Starts with Salesforce's Published Exam Domains
The foundation of every skills assessment at Transition Trails is Salesforce's official certification exam guide, published at help.salesforce.com. TT does not invent competency frameworks. We use Salesforce's own domain structure — with Salesforce's own weights — as the anchor for everything Penny asks, measures, and flags.

This makes every assessment defensible. When a participant, an employer, or a funder asks how we determined a learner's skill level, the answer is grounded in the same framework Salesforce uses to certify professionals worldwide.

The Non-Negotiable
All multiple choice questions, scenario prompts, and coach assessment criteria must be traceable to a specific Salesforce certification exam domain and a specific concept documented on help.salesforce.com or in Salesforce's official study guide. Penny does not quiz on TT-invented knowledge.
 | 

The Track Template — Applies to Every Future Track

Why Weights Matter for Assessment
A learner who fails Process Automation questions (16% of the exam) is at greater risk of exam failure than one who misses a Change Management question (7%). Penny prioritizes gap intervention by domain weight — not just by wrong answer count.
 | 

Layer 2 — The Capstone Map: Domain → Build → Acceptance Criteria
The Foundations Trail Traceability Matrix translates exam domains into specific capstone build tasks with documented acceptance criteria. This is the bridge between abstract knowledge and demonstrated skill.

Every build task in the HCH capstone is mapped to:
The specific exam domain it covers
The user story that defines the business requirement
The acceptance criteria that define pass/fail
The Definition of Done that confirms completion

Penny's scenario-based assessment questions are written directly from these acceptance criteria. When Penny asks a scenario question, it is not a generic Salesforce question — it is grounded in something the participant actually built.

Example — Process Automation Domain
User Story S2-05: Loop Protection Flow. Acceptance Criteria AC-1: An email with subject 'Automatic reply' must result in the Case being immediately set to Closed. Penny scenario prompt: 'Your Email-to-Case inbox is filling up with auto-generated replies from a client's out-of-office system. Cases are being created for each one. What Flow type, trigger, and action would you use to prevent this — and why would you choose After-Save instead of Before-Save?' This question cannot be answered correctly by guessing. It requires understanding the build they completed.

Mode 3 — Build Verification (Output Check)
Build verification confirms that the participant's org actually does what the Definition of Done requires. Penny cannot directly query the learner's Salesforce org — the participant reports the output, and Penny asks follow-up questions to verify understanding.

Sample Build Verification — Security Domain (Week 3)
Penny prompt: 'Log into your HCH org as Alex Rivera (Volunteer profile). Navigate to the Cases tab. Tell me exactly what you see.'Expected: 'Zero case records.' If the participant reports seeing cases, Penny asks: 'What does that tell you about your OWD configuration?' This surfaces whether the security architecture is actually working — not just configured.

SECTION 3 — THE COACH GATE
 | 

When Human Judgment Is Required
The coach gate is the point where Penny stops and a human takes over. There are three situations where the gate activates — and one situation where it is absolute.

Gate Trigger 1 — Domain Score Below Threshold
If a learner's cumulative domain score in any exam domain falls below 70% after the sprint that covers that domain, the gate activates.

Gate State
 | What It Means
 | 
ADVISORY (60–69%)
 | Penny flags the domain gap on the coach dashboard. Coach reviews and decides whether to intervene. Learner may proceed to next sprint while working on the gap.
 | 
BLOCKING (below 60%)
 | Learner cannot advance to the next sprint until the coach reviews their build artifacts and either clears the gate or assigns a remediation task. Penny notifies both learner and coach.
 | 
CRITICAL (below 50% on domains weighted 15%+)
 | Immediate 1:1 coach session required. Penny drafts the session agenda based on specific question-level failure data. Coach owns the outcome.
 | 

Gate Trigger 2 — Technically Correct, No Business Reasoning
This is the most important gate — and the hardest one for automated systems to catch alone.

A participant can answer every multiple choice question correctly and still not be ready. The red flag is a learner who can configure a solution but cannot explain it to Elena in plain English. Penny listens for this in scenario responses and flags it explicitly.

The Elena Test
The coach guide for every sprint uses the same standard: 'Can this learner explain their configuration decision to Elena — HCH's Program Director — without using jargon?' If the answer is no, the learner is following steps, not building understanding. Penny flags this pattern. The coach decides what to do about it.
 | 

Penny flags the Elena Test failure when a scenario response:
Contains the correct technical answer but no business justification
References Salesforce UI steps without explaining what the configuration achieves
Cannot connect the configuration choice to an HCH business outcome

These flags appear on the coach dashboard with the specific response text. The coach decides: advisory note, targeted follow-up prompt, or scheduled 1:1.

Gate Trigger 3 — Sprint 3 Exam Readiness
By Week 10, the Private Coach Session 2 framework includes a formal exam readiness check. This is never Penny's decision alone. The coach reviews:

Cumulative domain scores across all 9 domains
Scenario response quality from Sprints 1–3
Build verification completion rate
Admin Decision Log quality — are entries explaining the WHY, or just documenting the HOW
The learner's own confidence level in each domain

The coach's Session 2 recommendation is one of three outcomes: Ready to sit the exam, Needs 2–4 more weeks of focused prep in specific domains, or Needs a fundamental concept review before attempting the exam.

The Absolute Rule
Exam candidacy — the decision that a learner is ready to sit the Salesforce Administrator exam — is never Penny's decision. Penny surfaces the data. The coach makes the call. This is not a system limitation. It is a design principle.

The Friday Synthesis — Coach Intelligence Report
Every Friday, Gemini synthesizes the week's assessment data across the entire cohort into a coach intelligence report. This report tells the coach:

Which domains showed the most incorrect answers this week across the cohort
Which learners triggered advisory or blocking gates
Which learners haven't engaged with Penny in 4+ days
Elena Test failure patterns — are multiple learners struggling to explain the same concept
Suggested actions for Monday — specific, named, not generic

What the Coach Does With the Friday Report
The report is a to-do list, not a status update. If 3 of 8 learners failed the same Flow Builder scenario question, the coach doesn't intervene with 3 separate DMs — they open Monday's office hours with that scenario and let the group work through it together. The report turns individual gaps into cohort-level curriculum decisions.

SECTION 5 — APPLYING THE MODEL TO NEW TRACKS
 | 

The Template for Every New Track
When TT adds a new certification track — Agentforce, Slack, Advanced Admin, Product Owner — the same four-layer model applies. The only thing that changes is the content.

Step
 | What to Do
 | 
1 — Get the exam guide
 | Pull the official Salesforce certification exam outline from help.salesforce.com. Document every domain and its weight.
 | 
2 — Build the traceability matrix
 | Map every exam domain to a capstone build task. Use HCH or a DC client org scenario. Write acceptance criteria for each build in Given/When/Then format.
 | 
3 — Write the Penny content
 | For each domain: 10 multiple choice questions (MC), 3 scenario prompts tied to build tasks, 2 build verification checks. All grounded in help.salesforce.com.
 | 
4 — Set the gate thresholds
 | Confirm domain weight thresholds for advisory/blocking/critical gates. High-weight domains (15%+) have lower tolerance for gaps.
 | 
5 — Define the coach gate criteria
 | What scenarios require human review for this track? Document them explicitly. Client-facing tracks have stricter gates than exam-prep tracks.
 | 
6 — Load into Trail OS
 | Hugh loads the domain structure, question bank, and gate configurations into Trail OS. Penny inherits the track configuration automatically.
 | 

Agentforce & Slack — What Makes These Tracks Different
Agentforce and Slack certifications represent a significant growth opportunity for TT. Many participants will arrive already employed, wanting to add these skills. The assessment model is identical — but two things are different:

The capstone work is DC client work, not just HCH. Agentforce and Slack work happens inside real nonprofit orgs. This raises the coach gate standard — the client environment is not a practice org.
The skill gap profile is different. Participants arriving for Agentforce work often have strong admin fundamentals but no AI/automation exposure. The assessment needs to surface that gap early in Week 1, not Week 8.

The Agentforce Opportunity
Nonprofits are being asked about AI constantly and have almost no one to help them implement it responsibly. A ToM participant who completes the Agentforce track has something almost no one in the nonprofit Salesforce ecosystem has: supervised, documented, real-client Agentforce implementation experience. That is worth building for.

The Zero Fabrication Rule
Penny never invents a score, fabricates progress, or tells a learner they are ready when the data says otherwise. Every score is traceable to a specific question response or build verification result. Every flag has evidence behind it. This is not just an ethical standard — it is the foundation of the program's credibility with employers and funders.
