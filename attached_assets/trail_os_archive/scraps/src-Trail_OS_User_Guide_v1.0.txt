About This Document
This guide documents the architecture, configuration decisions, and operational standards for Trail OS — the integrated Salesforce-based operating system that runs Transition Trails. It covers Knowledge base structure, data category taxonomy, article types, related object relationships, and the Demand Management pipeline. This is a living document. When systems change, this document changes first.
## 1. Approach — How Trail OS Is Designed
Trail OS is the operating system for Transition Trails. It is not a collection of tools — it is a single, integrated platform built on Salesforce that manages program delivery, internal operations, client services, and organizational knowledge in one place.
## 1.1 Design Philosophy
Trail OS is built on three governing principles:
Single source of truth. Every process, procedure, article, and request has one authoritative home. Content does not live in multiple places.
Human in the loop. Nothing is published, deployed, or closed without explicit human confirmation. Angela owns the final gate on all Knowledge publishing and all Demand Management closures.
Architecture first. The category structure, object relationships, and article types were designed before content was created. This prevents the retrofit problem that most nonprofits face.

## 2.2 The Authoring and Publishing Workflow
Every article follows a seven-stage workflow from draft to live. Angela is the human gate at Stage 4 — nothing publishes without her explicit action.
Stage 1 — Draft in Notion
Author writes SOP in Notion using the standard template. Sets Status = Ready for Review when complete.
Stage 2 — Nightly Sync
Make automation detect Ready for Review. Creates a draft Knowledge article in Salesforce. Sets Notion Sync Status = Synced to SF - Pending Review.
Stage 3 — Angela Reviews
Angela opens the draft in Salesforce Knowledge. Reviews all content fields. Decides: publish as-is, edit then publish, or reject back to draft.
Stage 4 — Angela Publishes
Angela clicks Publish. Status moves to Online. This is the final human gate. Cannot be reversed without admin action.
Stage 5 — Article Live
Article immediately visible to all internal Salesforce users. Penny can surface it. Navigators see it if their profile has access.
Stage 6 — Review Cycle
Next Review Date triggers a reminder. Angela opens article, confirms accuracy, updates Last Reviewed Date.
Stage 7 — Archive
Angela archives outdated articles. Removed from active search. Admin action required to restore.
## 2.3 Naming Convention
Every article must follow this naming convention before it is created. Consistent naming makes search results readable and helps Penny surface the right article.

## 2.4 Article Review Standards
All published articles are reviewed on a schedule defined by the Review Frequency field. Default is Quarterly. Standards:
Quarterly — all Salesforce configuration SOPs. Platform changes with every release.
Monthly — any SOP touching an active automation or integration. These break silently.
Annually — stable reference articles, governance documents, HR policies.
As Needed — content library articles, Trail Kit resources that do not expire.
Sync Failure Protocol
If Notion Sync Status = Sync Failed appears on any article, check the Make automation run history immediately. Do not change the field manually until the root cause is identified. Log a Demand Management case with Change Type = Automation Change.

## 3.5 Visibility Rules
All three category groups are currently set to Internal Only. This is deliberate. The visibility expansion path is:
Internal Only — current state. Staff and contractors with Salesforce logins only.
Navigators — activated when Cohort 1 launches. Trail Kit and curriculum content become visible to authenticated Navigator portal users.
Nonprofit Clients — activated per client engagement. Client-specific SOPs and service delivery content.
Public — future state. Select best practice guides and program explainers visible to unauthenticated visitors on Experience Cloud.
Changing Visibility
Visibility changes are a Demand Management request — Change Category: Technical, Change Type: Configuration Change. Do not change Data Category visibility settings directly without first logging a case. Visibility changes affect what Penny surfaces and what Experience Cloud shows to unauthenticated visitors.

## 6. Related Objects — How Knowledge Connects to the Rest of Trail OS
Knowledge articles do not exist in isolation. They connect to Cases, Accounts, program objects, and Penny's action framework. Understanding these connections is essential for maintaining data integrity and for understanding how Penny surfaces content.
## 6.1 Object Relationship Map
Knowledge → Case
When a Demand Management case resolves, the closing SOP or article is linked via the Related Knowledge Article lookup field. Bug Fix cases also link to the Knowledge article that documents expected behavior.
Knowledge → Account
Client-specific SOPs and Trail Kit resources can be linked to the relevant nonprofit Account. This allows Penny to surface client-relevant content when working on a client Case.
Knowledge → Opportunity
Best practice guides and client deliverables can be linked to open Opportunities. Useful for pre-sales and discovery phases where content is part of the engagement.
Knowledge → Agentforce (Penny)
Penny uses the GetRelevantKnowledge action to surface articles. She routes by Applies To field (SOP) and Program Track + Audience tags (Curriculum Content). The Notion Sync Status must be Published for Penny to find the article.
Knowledge → Tango
Tango_URL__c on SOP articles links directly to the interactive walkthrough. Penny can surface just the Tango URL without pulling the full article — useful for quick Navigator guidance.
Knowledge → Notion
Notion is the draft environment. Notion_Page_ID__c is the dedup key that prevents duplicate articles on subsequent nightly syncs. Notion_Source_URL__c links back to the original draft for audit trail.

## 6.4 Penny — How She Uses Knowledge
Penny is the Agentforce AI agent that surfaces Knowledge content to Navigators, coaches, and clients in real time. Understanding how she works determines how articles should be structured and tagged.
How Penny finds articles
GetRelevantKnowledge Agentforce action. Searches title, summary, body, and tags. Filters by Applies To (SOP) and Audience tags (Curriculum Content). Only surfaces articles with Notion Sync Status = Published.
Client Request routing
When Change Category = Client Request, Penny pulls the Account record, surfaces prior cases for that client, and checks for related Opportunities before suggesting Knowledge articles.
Training Issue routing
When Change Type = Training Issue, Penny searches Knowledge and surfaces the top 3 matching articles. Recommends closing the case as a Training Issue and linking the relevant article.
Bug Report routing
Penny searches for prior bug cases on the same component. Surfaces the SOP documenting expected behavior. Suggests Root Cause Category based on description keywords.
Tango URL surfacing
Penny can surface just the Tango_URL__c field from a SOP article without returning the full procedure text. Used when a Navigator needs the interactive walkthrough rather than the written steps.
What breaks Penny
Articles without tags. Articles with Notion Sync Status ≠ Published. Articles with empty Applies To fields. Inconsistent naming convention makes title search unreliable.
## 7. Quick Reference — Kim's Operational Checklist
This section covers the most common Trail OS tasks Kim will perform and the correct steps for each.
## 7.1 When You Need to Create a New SOP
Write the draft in Notion using the standard SOP template.
Set the SF Data Category, SF Record Type = SOP, and Owner Department fields in the Notion template.
Set Notion Status = Ready for Review when the draft is complete.
Make automation runs nightly — article will appear in Salesforce Knowledge with status Synced to SF - Pending Review.
Notify Angela that an article is pending review.
Angela reviews, edits if needed, and publishes.
If the SOP is complex or cross-system
Log a Demand Management case first with Change Type = New SOP / Procedure and check Requires RESOLVE Evaluation. Complete the RESOLVE evaluation before writing the SOP. The SOP documents the outcome of RESOLVE — not the other way around.
## 7.2 When You Need to Submit a Change Request
Go to the Service Console → Cases → New → select Demand Management record type.
Select the correct Change Category from the dependent picklist.
Select the correct Change Type.
A check requires a RESOLVE Evaluation if the change is complex or cross-system.
Complete all required fields: Subject, Description, Business Justification, Expected Outcome, and Affected Systems.
Set Priority and save.
Angela reviews and sets the status to Intake Review.
## 7.3 When You Find a Bug
Log a Demand Management case with Change Category = Technical, Change Type = Bug Report.
Do NOT check Requires RESOLVE — bugs are post-RESOLVE by definition.
Complete Expected Behavior (what the system should do) and Actual Behavior (what it is doing).
Select Root Cause Category if known.
Check Knowledge Update Needed if the relevant SOP documents show incorrect behavior.
## 7.4 When a Knowledge Article Needs Updating
If the update is minor (typo, link fix, step clarification): edit directly in Salesforce Knowledge, update Last Reviewed Date, republish.
If the update reflects a process change, log a Demand Management case with Change Type = Process Change first. Complete the case, then update the article.
If the update is triggered by a bug fix, the Bug Report case should have Knowledge Update Needed checked. Update the article as part of case closure.
Always update the Last Reviewed Date and Last Tested Version when making substantive changes.

## 8. Governance — Keeping Trail OS Healthy
Trail OS is only as useful as the discipline behind it. These are the standing governance rules that keep the system clean, searchable, and trustworthy.
## 8.1 The Non-Negotiables
Angela is the only person who publishes Knowledge articles. No exceptions.
No article is created without a Data Category assigned.
No article is created without following the naming convention.
No process or system change happens without a Demand Management case.
No new tags are created without a Demand Management request.
Notion Sync fields (Page ID, Source URL, Sync Status) are never edited manually.
RESOLVE evaluation is never skipped for technical changes, new programs, new products, or new campaigns.

## 8.3 When Something Is Wrong
Sync failure (Notion Sync Status = Sync Failed): check Make run history → log Demand Management case → do not publish manually.
Article content is wrong: do not delete. Edit and republish. Log the reason in Lessons Learned if a case was involved.
Wrong Data Category assigned: correct it directly in the article. No case needed for metadata corrections.
Wrong article type used: archive the article and recreate it with the correct record type. Do not attempt to change the record type on an existing article.
Tag not in the approved list: use the closest-matching approved tag and log a Demand Management case to add the new tag if genuinely needed.
