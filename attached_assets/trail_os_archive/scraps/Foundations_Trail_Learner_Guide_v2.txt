1. Program Overview
The Foundations Trail is a 12-week practitioner program for people who have earned their Salesforce Platform Foundations certification and are ready to take the next step: passing the Salesforce Administrator exam and building the experience that gets them hired.

This is not a study course. Every week you build something real for a nonprofit — Horizon Community Hub (HCH) in the Dallas-Fort Worth area. Every artifact you build goes into a professional portfolio on Salesforce Experience Cloud. By Week 12, you have a working org, a published portfolio, and 12 weeks of documented decisions that prove you think like an admin.

The Prerequisite Is Not Negotiable
Platform Foundations Certification — Required Before Week 1
 | 
You must hold the Salesforce Platform Foundations certification before you begin. This is not a gatekeeping formality. The Foundations Trail builds directly on that knowledge. Learners without the cert fall behind by Week 2 and cannot recover pace. If you do not yet have Platform Foundations, earn it first — then enroll.

The Admin Decision Log
The Admin Decision Log is a running document of every significant configuration decision you make — and why you made it. You start it in Week 2 and add at least one entry every week. By Week 12 you have a minimum of 12 documented decisions.

Format for every entry: What did I decide? What were the alternatives? Why did I choose this option? What would have broken if I had chosen differently?

Why the Decision Log matters
 | 
The exam does not test whether you can click through a setup menu. It tests whether you understand why you make certain configuration choices. The Decision Log builds that habit. In an interview, "Here is my Decision Log" is worth more than any certification badge.

NPSP — What It Changes and Why HCH Needs It
In standard Salesforce, a Contact links to a business Account. In NPSP, a Contact links to a Household Account — an Account type that represents a family unit. When you create a Contact named "Carlos Martinez," NPSP automatically creates a "Martinez Household" Account and links Carlos to it.

HCH serves families, not individuals. The Martinez family — Carlos, Maria, and their daughter Sofia — must all be linked under one Household so Elena can see the complete family service history in one place. Without NPSP, that family relationship does not exist in the standard data model.

🎯  Exam Connection
 | 
Configuration & Setup is 20% of the Administrator exam — the highest single domain weight. The exam does not test whether you can click through setup menus. It tests whether you understand WHAT each setting controls and WHAT HAPPENS if it is wrong. Key concept this week: the difference between org-level settings (affect all users) and object-level settings (field and record level). That distinction is the conceptual backbone of Sprint 1.

The Four Custom Fields — With Reasoning
Field
 | Type
 | Why This Type
 | Why NOT the Alternative
 | 
Utility Account Number
 | Text, length 18
 | Utility account numbers like "UTL-88421" contain letters and leading zeros. Text preserves them exactly.
 | Number strips the "UTL-" prefix and leading zeros. "088421" becomes "88421" — permanently wrong and unrecoverable.
 | 
Lease Expiration Date
 | Date
 | A calendar date. No time component needed.
 | Date/Time adds unnecessary timestamps. Text loses date filtering and formula compatibility.
 | 
Veteran Status
 | Picklist: Yes / No / Unknown
 | Controlled values enable reliable filtering. The grant eligibility report filters by Veteran = "Yes" — that only works with exact values.
 | Text: users type "yes," "YES," "Veteran," "y." The report count breaks. Checkbox: cannot store "Unknown" — only Yes/No.
 | 
Income Bracket
 | Picklist: 5 tiers
 | Controlled categories for grant eligibility reporting. Brackets, not exact amounts.
 | Number or Currency: exact amounts are not how grant eligibility works. Text: same reliability problem as Veteran Status.
 | 

The Validation Rule
Formula: AND(NOT(ISBLANK(Lease_Expiration_Date__c)), Lease_Expiration_Date__c < TODAY())
Error message: "Lease Expiration Date must be today or a future date."
Error location: Field (not top of page — appears next to the problem field).

⚠️  Watch Out — Avoid These Mistakes
 | 
Set Required = FALSE on all four fields at the field level. Some clients will not have a utility account number or a lease date yet. If you make any field required at the API level, your data import in Week 4 will fail for every record missing that value — 11 of the 36 clean records have no utility account number.
 | 
The ISBLANK() guard in the validation rule is not optional. Without it, the formula fires on every new Contact with a blank Lease Expiration Date — because blank evaluates to null, which is less than TODAY(). Result: you cannot create any new Contact records at all. Always wrap: AND(NOT(ISBLANK(...)), your_condition).
 | 
Veteran Status must be a Picklist, not a Text field. The grant eligibility report in Week 4 filters by Veteran Status = "Yes." A Text field returns unreliable counts because users type it differently every time.
 | 
"Required on the page layout" and "Required at the field level" are two different things. Page layout required = the user sees an asterisk and cannot save via the UI. Field-level required = the import API cannot create the record. Know which one you are setting and why.
 | 

🎯  Exam Connection
 | 
Object Manager & Lightning App Builder is 20% of the exam — tied for highest. This week covers the most tested sub-topics: field type selection, validation rule logic, and page layout design. The exam gives scenarios: "A nonprofit needs to track veteran status for grant reporting — which field type?" The answer is Picklist, and the reason is what the exam actually tests: controlled values make reporting reliable.

The Four Security Layers — In Order
Layer
 | Controls
 | HCH Configuration
 | Exam Concept
 | 
Profiles / Permission Sets
 | What objects and fields a user can access — regardless of which records they own
 | Volunteer Profile: no Case access, Veteran Status and Income Bracket hidden. Support Staff: full Case access, all fields visible.
 | Profiles set the floor. Permission Sets add to it. One profile per user; multiple permission sets possible.
 | 
Organization-Wide Defaults (OWD)
 | The baseline record visibility for the entire org
 | Case OWD = Private. Only the record owner and users above them in the hierarchy can see a Case.
 | OWD is the most tested security concept. Private = restrictive default. Everything else opens access from there.
 | 
Role Hierarchy
 | Opens visibility upward above the OWD baseline
 | Executive Director > Program Director > Support Staff > Volunteer. Elena can see all Support Staff cases.
 | Hierarchy grants upward visibility. Tom Reid's manager Elena sees Tom's cases. Tom cannot see Elena's.
 | 
Sharing Rules
 | Extends access sideways to users at the same level
 | Not configured this week — covered in the Guided Trail.
 | Sharing Rules exist to handle horizontal access that hierarchy does not cover.
 | 

What You Are Building
Build the HCH Role Hierarchy: Executive Director > Program Director > Support Staff > Volunteer
Clone Standard User profile to create the HCH Volunteer Profile with reduced permissions
Set Case OWD to Private
Apply FLS: Veteran Status and Income Bracket hidden from Volunteer profile, visible to Support Staff
Create 5 test users from the Data Guide
Run the acceptance tests: log in as each test user, verify access exactly matches expectations

⚠️  Watch Out — Avoid These Mistakes
 | 
Never test security as System Admin. System Admin bypasses all security — there is nothing to verify. "It looks like it's working" tested as yourself means nothing. Log in as each test user individually. That is the only valid test.
 | 
Create all 5 test users before running any security tests. You cannot log in as a user that does not exist.
 | 
Volunteer must be a child (below) of Support Staff in the role hierarchy — not at the same level. If they are at the same level, Volunteers gain peer visibility into Support Staff records, which breaks the privacy model.
 | 
FLS is set on the Profile, not the Role. A common mistake is setting FLS on the Role (which does not exist — roles do not control field visibility). Go to the Volunteer Profile > Field-Level Security > hide the fields there.
 |

🎯  Exam Connection
 | 
Security & Access is 13% of the exam. The critical mental model: Salesforce security is additive at the object/field level (profiles and permission sets grant access) but restrictive at the record level (OWD locks down by default; Role Hierarchy and Sharing Rules open it back up). The most tested question type: "A caseworker should see their own cases but not their colleague's. Which feature controls this?" Answer: OWD set to Private — not Profiles, not the Role Hierarchy.

Sprint 2 — Sales & Marketing Applications
 | Exam Weight
~12%
of Admin Exam

What You Are Building
Build ONE Case Assignment Rule with multiple entries routing by Record Type to correct queues
Add Email-to-Case routing addresses for help@hch.org (Housing) and grants@hch.org (Veteran Services)
Create Auto-Response Rules with queue-specific email templates for each Record Type
Test the full chain: submit via each channel, verify correct queue landing and auto-response delivery
Add Decision Log Entry 7: Why Record Type routing over Subject keyword routing?

⚠️  Watch Out — Avoid These Mistakes
 | 
Only ONE Assignment Rule can be active per object. Activating a second rule immediately deactivates the first. All your routing logic must live inside a single rule as multiple entries. If you create separate rules thinking they compound, your routing will silently break.
 | 
Rule entries evaluate top-down — first match wins. Entry 1: Utility Assistance → Utility Queue. Entry 2: Housing Support → Housing Queue. Entry 3: Veteran Services → Veteran Queue. If a case matches Entry 1 criteria, it never reaches Entry 2 even if Entry 2 also matches.
 | 
Auto-Response Rules should fire on Origin = Web OR Email only — not on internally created cases. If you do not include an Origin filter, every case created manually by a caseworker will also trigger an auto-response email to the client. This is confusing and unprofessional.
 |

What You Are Building
Build a Record-Triggered Flow on Case that fires on Case Created (After-Save)
Decision element: Subject CONTAINS "Out of Office," "Automatic reply," or "Undeliverable" (ANY match)
Update Records element: sets Status = "Closed" for matched cases
Test with a loop-trigger case AND a legitimate case — verify only the right one closes
Record a 3-minute Loom walkthrough of the Flow logic
Add Decision Log Entry 8: After-Save vs. Before-Save — why did you choose what you chose?

Why This Flow Exists
When a client's email address has an out-of-office auto-reply active, every case-creation notification HCH sends generates another auto-reply from the client, which Email-to-Case converts into another new case, which sends another notification, which generates another reply. Without loop protection, one client interaction creates dozens of phantom cases and floods HCH's queue.

⚠️  Watch Out — Avoid These Mistakes
 | 
Test 1 must trigger a close. Test 2 must NOT. Subject = "Automatic reply: Re: your utility request" should auto-close. Subject = "Help, my lights are out" must remain open. If both close, your Decision element conditions are wrong — likely the condition value field was left blank, which evaluates all cases as a match.
 | 
CONTAINS is case-insensitive in Flow — "out of office" and "OUT OF OFFICE" both match. You do not need separate conditions for different capitalizations.
 | 
The Decision element must evaluate ANY match (not ALL). If you configure it as ALL conditions must be true, a subject of "Automatic reply: Out of Office" would need to contain all three phrases simultaneously — which it never will.
 | 

🎯  Exam Connection
 | 
Flow Builder is the most important automation topic on the current Administrator exam. The exam has shifted heavily toward Flow since the retirement of Workflow Rules and Process Builder. Key concepts: Record-Triggered Flow types (Before Save vs. After Save — Before Save is faster, no extra DML, cannot access related records; After Save can update related records and send notifications), and the trigger types (Created, Updated, Created or Updated, Deleted).

The Key Technical Details
Element
 | What It Does
 | Why It Matters
 | 
OwnerId STARTS WITH "00G"
 | Filters to Cases still owned by a queue (Queue IDs start with 00G; User IDs start with 005)
 | This is the only reliable way to detect "no caseworker has claimed this yet" — because once a caseworker takes the case, the OwnerId becomes their User ID starting with 005.
 | 
CreatedDate <= NOW - (1/12)
 | 1/12 of a day = 2 hours. Filters to Cases at least 2 hours old.
 | Testing: change to (1/288) for 5-minute threshold. Restore to (1/12) before going live.
 | 
Private Bell Notification
 | A private in-app notification — no Chatter post, no email blast
 | Elena specifically requested no public post. A Chatter post would appear on the Case record visible to all queue members, potentially embarrassing staff.
 | 

⚠️  Watch Out — Avoid These Mistakes
 | 
Elena is receiving alerts for Cases her team already responded to. Most likely cause: a caseworker responded but did not change the Status away from "New." The Flow checks Status = New — if Status is not updated, the alert fires even after a response. This is a process gap, not a configuration error. Document it in the Decision Log.
 | 
Reduce the time threshold to 5 minutes for testing (change 1/12 to 1/288). Verify Elena's notification fires. Then restore to 2 hours before leaving the Flow active. Running a 30-minute scheduled Flow at 5-minute threshold in a real org generates excessive execution logs.
 | 
You must create the Custom Notification Type in Setup before using it in the Flow. Go to Setup > Notification Builder > Custom Notifications. Without this, the Send Notification action in Flow has nothing to reference.
