1. Program Overview

1.1 What Is the Guided Trail?
The Guided Trail (GT) is a 12-week, cohort-based practicum designed for learners who have already passed the Salesforce Administrator certification exam but have not yet been hired into their first Salesforce admin role. It is the next step on the Transition Trails learning path after the Admin Certification Trail (ACT).

The Guided Trail is not a course. It is not a review program. It is a professional proving ground.

In the Guided Trail, learners stop following instructions and start making decisions. Every week, you receive a stakeholder brief from Elena, HCH’s Program Director, and your job is to figure out how to build the solution yourself. No walkthrough videos. No step-by-step scripts. Just a business problem, a Salesforce org, and a cohort of peers working through the same challenge alongside you.

The artifacts you produce become your proof — a documented portfolio of real administrative judgment that employers can review, evaluate, and trust.

The Guided Trail Difference: Problem-Solving Over Procedure
ACT taught you what to build and showed you how. The Guided Trail gives you a problem and asks you to decide.
Employers don’t need admins who follow tutorials. They need admins who troubleshoot, prioritize, and document their reasoning.
The Guided Trail is where you develop — and prove — that kind of judgment.
 | 

1.2 Who This Program Is For
The Guided Trail is exclusively for learners who meet all three of the following criteria:

You hold the Salesforce Administrator certification
You have not yet been hired for your first Salesforce Admin role
You are committed to a 12-week cohort-based experience with live participation

This program is designed for the learner who has done everything “right” — passed the exam, built Trailhead badges, applied for jobs — and still cannot get past the experience requirement. The Guided Trail exists to close that gap with verified, portfolio-ready proof.

Who Is Not Ready for the Guided Trail
You have not yet passed the Salesforce Administrator exam. (ACT is your next step.)
You are already employed as a Salesforce Admin. (Trail of Mastery is your next step.)
You cannot commit to live weekly sessions. Session replays are a backup for life events, not a replacement for participation.

4.1 Weekly Trail Map — Sprint 1

Week 1: Mapping the Chaos — Org Audit & Data Architecture Review
Stakeholder Brief: Elena has pulled in a new volunteer coordinator who is trying to understand how client data is organized in Salesforce. She’s receiving conflicting answers from staff. Elena needs you to produce a clear, one-page overview of HCH’s data architecture that anyone on the team can understand.
Build Task: Conduct a full org audit. Review the HCH data model: all custom objects, relationships, key fields, and field dependencies. Identify any configuration gaps, duplicate fields, or naming inconsistencies left over from the initial build. Produce an Entity Relationship Diagram (ERD) for the current HCH data model.
Portfolio Artifact: Publish your ERD to your portfolio with a 150-word written explanation of why HCH uses the Household Account model and how it serves their family-based mission.
Penny Check-In: Ask Penny: ‘If a hiring manager asked me to walk them through a Salesforce data model I’d built, what should I be ready to explain? Give me five questions they might ask.’ Practice your answers.
Decision Log Entry: #GT-01: What did your audit reveal? What is working well, and what would you change if given the authority to do so? Why?

Week 2: Secure Foundations — Security Audit & Access Review
Stakeholder Brief: Elena has been informed that HCH recently onboarded two new part-time staff members. She wants to confirm that each staff member can see only what they need to see — and that no volunteer has access to confidential client financial data.
Build Task: Perform a complete security audit of the HCH org. Review profiles, permission sets, OWD settings, role hierarchy, and sharing rules. Identify any over-permissioned users, gaps in field-level security, or sharing rules that expose data inappropriately. Remediate any issues you find.
Portfolio Artifact: Document your security audit findings: what you found, what you changed, and your business justification for each change. Minimum 3 documented remediation actions.

7.2 The Admin Decision Log as a Portfolio Centerpiece
The Admin Decision Log is the single most differentiating artifact in your Guided Trail portfolio. Employers who have reviewed Transition Trails portfolios consistently return to the Decision Log — because it proves something a certification cannot: that you think like an admin, not just a test-taker.

A complete Guided Trail Decision Log contains:

Minimum 12 documented decisions, one per week (GT-01 through GT-12)
For each entry: the business context, the options you considered, the option you chose, and the business justification
At least 3 entries that document a mistake or unexpected outcome — with what you learned and what you would do differently
Language that Elena (a non-technical stakeholder) could read and understand
At least 2 entries that explicitly reference a stakeholder preference or constraint that shaped your decision

The Decision Log in a Job Interview
When a hiring manager asks ‘Have you ever made a configuration decision you later had to revisit?’, your Decision Log has the answer.
When they ask ‘How do you document your work?’, your Decision Log is the answer.
When they ask ‘Can you walk me through a real business problem you solved in Salesforce?’, your Decision Log is the foundation of your answer.
The Decision Log is not documentation for documentation’s sake. It is the artifact that makes your portfolio tell a story.

The Guided Trail is the most focused program in the Transition Trails portfolio. Every design decision — the removal of walkthroughs, the emphasis on Decision Log documentation, the embedded interview prep, the employer simulation in Demo Day — exists to answer one question: Why can’t a certified Salesforce Admin get hired?

The answer Transition Trails has identified: They lack proof of judgment, not proof of configuration ability.

The Guided Trail is the proof engine.

9.2 Instructional Design Principles
The Guided Trail is designed on four core instructional principles that differentiate it from traditional Salesforce training:

Principle
 | Why It Matters
 | 
Problem-First Learning
 | Every build task begins with a stakeholder brief describing a business problem. The learner must figure out the solution. This mirrors how real admin work arrives — as a problem, not as a tutorial.
 | 
Decision Documentation as a Learning Artifact
 | The Admin Decision Log is not an administrative requirement. It is a learning artifact. The act of documenting decisions — including options rejected and mistakes made — builds metacognitive awareness that certification exams do not test.
 | 
Peer Learning Through Campfire Sessions
 | The Campfire Session is the pedagogical core of the program. Learners who built the same task present different solutions. Hearing other approaches, defending your own, and receiving critique from peers is how practitioners develop professional judgment.
 | 
Career Translation as a Required Skill
 | Knowing how to build something in Salesforce and knowing how to talk about having built it are two different skills. The Guided Trail treats career translation — the ability to explain technical work in business language — as a required curriculum outcome, not an optional soft skill.
 | 

9.3 Cohort Design
Guided Trail cohorts are intentionally kept small. Cohorts of 8–12 learners allow every participant to present work at Campfire Sessions, receive coach attention in Stand-Ups, and build meaningful peer relationships over 12 weeks.

Cohort Size
 | 8–12 learners (minimum 6 to run, maximum 12 for quality)
 | 
Cohort Composition
 | Mixed experience backgrounds encouraged. Career starters, career shifters, and experienced pros in the same cohort produces richer Campfire discussions.
 | 
Coach Ratio
 | 1 coach per cohort for Campfire Sessions and Stand-Ups. Private coaching sessions may be delivered by a different coach with appropriate handoff documentation.
 | 
Session Recordings
 | All group sessions are recorded. Recordings are made available within 24 hours. Attendance policy: recordings are a backup, not a replacement.
 | 
Cohort Communication
 | Primary channel: Slack (dedicated cohort channel). Secondary: program platform. Coach responds to Slack messages within 48 hours on weekdays.
