What carries across

Eleven stations, one continuous record

Nothing restarts at a program boundary. That is the whole design — a learner is not enrolling over and over, they are walking one trail, and the evidence accumulates the entire way.

The Decision Log
Entry #1 is written in Week 1 of Foundations. It is at #37 by the end of the apprenticeship, numbered continuously and never restarted. Context, options, the choice, the business reason.
The vocabulary
Sprint, Definition of Done, Pass, Needs rework, acceptance criteria. Taught once in the first two weeks and identical in every program, so no transition costs a week of relearning.
RESOLVE
Recognize · Explore · Select · Outline · Launch · Verify · Evolve. Introduced in the Practitioner Series, taught as a module in Guided Trail, and the method the apprenticeship runs on end to end.
The public portfolio
One shareable page she owns for as long as she stays involved — hosted by Transition Trails, not trapped in a sandbox that expires. Decisions she defended, systems she inherited and improved, her 90-second answers. Recruiters, partners, anyone she chooses.
Explorer's Trail
Not a stage between programs — the connective tissue underneath all of them. Earned at Guided Trail completion and never given up, it is where she waits for a role, brings real questions once she has one, and stays involved between engagements.
The coach ladder
Coach's Assistant, then Associate Coach, then Advanced Coach — open to anyone in Explorer's Trail. Judging the cohort behind her is how her own standard sharpens, and it is how coaching capacity grows without being recruited from outside.
Expedition Weekend
Twice a year, every program, one sealed brief opened together on a clock — one virtual, one in person in Dallas. A learner in month two and a coach in year three build side by side, and recruiters watch the work happen instead of reading about it.
Penny
Primary coach in Foundations, the interviewer in Guided Trail, the goal-setter in Explorer's Trail. One AI coach who has read everything the learner has ever submitted.
The capability

We can build a track for a skill that does not have one yet

Marisol's Agentforce apprenticeship is not a special case — it is the model working as designed. When the ecosystem moves faster than curricula can, we assemble a custom coaching program around the capability someone actually needs: a real nonprofit client engagement for the judgment, and skill development modules underneath it so the fundamentals are never assumed. The programs evolve because the community tells us what to build next.

A real client engagement
A nonprofit that needs the same capability, supervised by a coach. Judgment only forms where there are consequences — a lab exercise cannot produce a stakeholder who changes their mind in week six.
Modules underneath it
Skill development modules run alongside the engagement so fundamentals are covered rather than assumed. The client sets the work; the modules make sure nothing load-bearing gets skipped because the client never happened to need it.
Coverage decides the rest
A minimum skill set is named up front, and the coverage ledger tracks what has actually been exercised. Prepared use cases fill anything the client never produced — so a custom track is still a track, not an improvisation.
Certification bodies teach what is already stable. Bootcamps teach what was in demand when the curriculum was written. A supervised client engagement wrapped in fundamentals can be assembled for a capability that is six months old — and the learner who commissions it often ends up helping write the track they are taking.
