01 · Why it exists

A membership, not a course — and the supply side of the model

Digital Compass serves nonprofit organizations directly. It is a recurring relationship rather than a program with a graduation date: weekly office hours, a library of resources built from real client work, and the ability to submit real Salesforce projects that coached learners complete. Transition Trails has been supporting nonprofits this way since 2022 — CLYLP first, then Hartford Promise, and Global One Urban Farming most recently — and all three are still clients. The membership structure names and prices what was already happening.

Trail of Mastery runs on live nonprofit engagements, and that work does not appear on its own — it arrives through Digital Compass intake. The number of Mid and Full tier members is a direct ceiling on how many apprenticeships can run. Guided Trail is deliberately insulated from that: it works Transition Trails\u2019 own backlog and takes nonprofit overflow only when it exists, so its cohorts are not gated on the membership count. Growing Digital Compass is what unlocks Trail of Mastery — and every Mid or Full member added is another apprenticeship that can be staffed.

02 · The front door

Base Camp — we set up your Power of Us org, free

Salesforce donates ten licenses to eligible nonprofits through the Power of Us program. It is an extraordinary grant and most recipients never really claim it — the licenses are approved, nobody knows what to do next, and the org sits empty or half-configured for a year. Transition Trails does that setup pro bono: Nonprofit Cloud with Agentforce, whichever NPSP feature sets the organization actually needs, and a check on the Slack, Google and Canva grants while we are there. It is a small piece of work for us and the difference between a dormant grant and a working system for them.

While we are in there — the rest of the grant stack

Power of Us is the one worth the setup effort, but it is not the only program a nonprofit qualifies for and never claims. An organization eligible for one is almost always eligible for the others, and checking costs us a conversation. It widens Base Camp from a Salesforce favor into something closer to a technology grant audit — which is a better reason to say yes and a better story for a funder.

Start with what Power of Us already reaches
Ten donated seats is the part everyone knows. The entitlement runs wider than that — Data Cloud, Commerce Cloud, Marketing Cloud, Tableau, Experience Cloud, Slack and more sit behind it at donated or heavily discounted nonprofit rates, and the terms move often enough that even engaged organizations lose track. Almost nobody reads the whole thing. A license review is an hour of reading against a current entitlement and it routinely finds capability an organization has been quoting external vendors for.
Two things follow from it. It is the most credible possible opening — we tell a nonprofit something true and valuable about their own account before asking for anything. And it generates qualified project work: an organization that discovers it already has Experience Cloud has a reason to talk about a portal, and that conversation belongs in the project catalogue rather than with a consultancy.

The catalogue already covers most of this. Google Workspace, Google Sites, Gmail and Calendar setup are four existing project types; Slack setup is a fifth. Grant activation is not new work to design — it is existing project types pointed at a new trigger, which is why the audit can be free and the follow-through can be a paid project.
The Master Program Guide has an open item about what a nonprofit is told when learners work in their production org. Base Camp sidesteps it entirely: a freshly granted Power of Us org has no data, no users, no configuration debt and nothing to break. It is the lowest-risk real work in the entire Academy, and it is real — a live org for a live organization, not a scenario. That makes it the natural first client engagement for a Guided Trail squad, months before anyone goes near a system somebody depends on.
Why give it away
Because the hard part of selling a nonprofit membership is the first conversation, and this replaces it with a delivered result. An organization whose org we have just stood up has already been helped once, at no risk and no cost, and the obvious next question is who keeps it running. Base Camp is not marketing spend dressed as generosity — it is the cheapest client acquisition available to us, and it produces a working Salesforce org for a nonprofit either way.

04 · Tiers

What separates the tiers is project access, then the roadmap

Every tier gets the office hours and the library. Mid buys the two annual reviews and the ability to submit project work — which is also where a member starts generating learner placements. Full is where those reviews become a written personalised roadmap, alongside priority queue placement and a named point of contact. Annual is ten times monthly, two months free, and is the form grant-funded purchases should take.

05 · The pipeline

Where a nonprofit problem becomes a learner's evidence

This is the exchange the whole Academy runs on. It is also the place where the programs have to agree with each other about who is allowed to talk to whom — and right now they do not quite.

{{ e.prog }} {{ e.link }}
The Digital Compass blueprint says Guided Trail learners complete supervised project work for member nonprofits and calls it the primary learner-client pipeline. The Master Program Guide says there is no direct client contact until Trail of Mastery. Both are true, and the reconciliation is the word contact. Guided Trail learners do real work for real member organizations — in a sandbox, never in production, and never in a room with the member. The coach relays every requirement and carries every result back. At Trail of Mastery the learner is in front of the client and the coach is present rather than in between.

The reason is coaching, not liability. A coach has to be able to stop a learner mid-decision and correct them, and there is no way to do that while a client watches without undermining both of them. Every document, and the public site, should say it in exactly those terms.

06 · Project work

Eighteen project types, each with a timeframe and a value

A member picks a category, then a type; the timeframe is calculated and a project manager is assigned. Because every type carries an estimated market value, completed project work converts directly into the impact figure a funder asks for — and into the "for every $1 granted" leverage line the grant framing already uses.

07 · Capstone

Practise in Horizon. Do it for real in yours.

Horizon Community Hub is the fictional Dallas–Fort Worth nonprofit used across the learner programs. In Foundations a learner builds its system. In the Digital Compass capstone the member is the person who has to use it — the program director or executive director who needs to know what to ask and how to tell whether the answer is right. It is the one place in the Academy where the learner-side and client-side curricula share a scenario, which makes it unusually cheap to maintain.

All four modules are complete for both roles and can be sold as an included benefit rather than a coming feature. What is not settled is the stakeholder material — the personas a member interacts with inside the scenario — which needs to be run with real members before it is fixed. That is a testing question, not a build one, and it does not hold the capstone back.

The rule that makes own-org work safe
Every own-org task in the capstone is read, document, or recommend — never change. A member investigates their duplicates and writes the cleanup plan; they do not run the merge. They scope the new module; they do not build it. That is not a limitation dressed up as pedagogy: the recommendation is the skill this program teaches, and the build is what a project submission is for. It also means a member can do all four modules in production without a sandbox, on their own time, with nothing at risk.
Office hours becomes the review venue. A member brings their own-org output and gets it read by someone who has seen a hundred of them — which gives the weekly session a spine without turning it into a class, and gives the member a reason to attend that is specifically theirs.
Where module four landsThe last own-org output is a technology decision memo about a change the organization is actually considering — which is, structurally, a scoped project request. A member finishes the capstone holding the exact document the project submission form asks for. That is the cleanest path from Entry tier to Mid that exists in this program, and it arrives because the member did the thinking, not because anyone sold them anything.
08 · Measures

Ten figures, and none of them belong in the learner dashboard

Digital Compass measures organizations; the learner programs measure people. The Master Program Guide's rule about never summing incomparable figures applies here more than anywhere — a Digital Compass completion rate and a Guided Trail pass rate have nothing to do with each other and must sit in separate panels.

The one figure that ties the two halves together
Learner Pipeline — the share of Digital Compass project slots filled by Guided Trail or Trail of Mastery learners rather than by staff. It is the only measure in either half of the Academy that fails if the other half is not working, which makes it the honest health check on the whole model. It should appear on the learner dashboard and the client one.
